## What Is It?

**Food cost** tells you what each dish *costs you to make* and how much *margin* it leaves —
computed automatically from its **recipe** and your **stock prices**. It's how you find the
dishes quietly eating your profit.

<span id="section-2" />

### How It Works (Simply)

The number comes straight from the chain you already set up:

```
Stock prices  ─┐
               ├─→  Recipe (ingredients + preparations × quantities)  ─→  Dish cost  ─→  Margin vs its price
Prep costs    ─┘
```

So a dish only has a food cost once it has a [recipe](/en/help/menu/ingredients-and-portions).
No recipe = no cost (and the dish shows as un-costed).

<span id="section-3" />

## What you see

Under Kitchen → Food Cost, inspect ingredient cost excluding VAT, selling price, cost percentage and margin for priced dishes. The chart has a 35% reference line; this is not an editable target on this page. Use search, status filter and sorting to inspect dishes. Missing recipe quantities or reliable costs prevent a reliable comparison.

<span id="section-4" />

## How to use it

- **Sort by food cost %** to find your worst offenders first.
- **A dish costs too much?** Adjust its portion or ingredients on the [Recipe and cost section](/en/help/menu/ingredients-and-portions), change its price, or substitute a cheaper ingredient.
- **Prices changed?** Update the ingredient in [Stock](/en/help/kitchen/stock-management); every dish using it re-costs automatically.

<span id="section-5" />

## Theoretical vs actual

The Food cost page is your **theoretical** cost — what each dish *should* cost. To compare it
against what you **actually** used (waste, over-portioning, errors), close the day in
[Daily operations](/en/help/kitchen/daily-operations); the variance is where money leaks.
